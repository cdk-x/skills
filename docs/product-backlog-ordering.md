# Product Backlog Ordering

The Product Backlog replace the workd "prioritize" with "order". The reason for this is that "priority" was too often equated with business value and categorizations such as High, Medium, Low or MosCow (Must, Should, Could, Won't)

While business priority is important, it is not the only variable that affects the order in which you pull things off the Product Backlog

To properly order your Product Backlog, you need to consider manu aspects:

- **Business Value**: The value created from implementing the feature: reveneu, cost saving, customer retention, propect customer, and future opportunity. Features that map closets to the product vision likely rank highest here.
- **Risk**: The importance of the Product Backlog item in terms of exposure to a harfmull situation. This includes both business and techincal risk. The higher the risk, the higher it should be in the Product Backlog.
- **Cost/Size**: The cost of implementing a feature. This is mostly (but not exclusverly) associated with the effort and time that de Development Team needs to build it.
- **Dependency**: Regardless of the value, risk, and cost/size, sometime a feature cannot be done before another. These can be both business and organizational dependencies.

As you can see, many forces are at play. Consider the following formula:

(Business Value + Risk) / Size = Order Rank

By findeing a way to enumerate each of these variables, you can create an order ranking system in which the higher the number, the higher the item will be inthe Product Backlog. Then make adjustments based on indentified dependencies.

By focusing on smaller valueable features, you risk that the large yet valueable item get ignored.

Ordering the Product Backlog will open up many important conversations within your Team that will clarify assumptions, misconceptions, and dependencies and thereby reduce accidental complexity. This process itself generates lots of value.

## Measuring Value, Risk and Size.

### Value
If you can determine the monetary value amount of the Product Backlog item, then that would be ideal. However, it is rarely that easy.

Other approaches uses somewhat arbitrary numbers to indicate value, much like with *relative effort sizing*. The number range does not matter as much as getting stakeholders engaged and using the wisdom of the crowd. These are plenty of facilitation techniques for this:

- **Business Value Game**. Using planning poker to estimate value instead of size.
- **Buy-a-feature**. Innovation Game using money to purchase features.
- **20/20 Vision**. Innovate Game for simple ordering of a Product Backlog.
- **Thirty-Five**. Colaboration activity.

Using these democratic and inclusive process with stakeholders for assiging business value to Product Backlog items has two main benefits for Product Owners:

1. They get a better overall sense of what their stakeholders are thingking.
2. Their stakeholders feel more included and heard.

### Risk

The easiest way to measure the risk is with a simple Low, Medium, High ranking provided by the Team. To use this system in the formula, assign a number to each risk rating (e.g. L=1, M=5, H=10). Ultimaterly, the scale you use to represent risk depends on other factors: How important is risk for your product? What scale are you using for value?

Risk may not even need to be considered for certain products, while others may want to give a risk more weight and more ranges.

### Size

The most common scale to use when assigning relative suze to estimate the Product Backlog item is the Fibonacci sequence (1,2,3,5,8,13, etc). For the purpose of ordering a Product Backlog, just know that the size of an item is a factor. However, what you name the unit that represents its size, is not that importante (often is called a point or a story point)

Keep in mind that this formula does not take dependencies into account. It may be good idea to have you Team make a pass over the ordered Product Backlog to reorder based on technical and business dependencies.

Refinement is a great oppportunity to revisit this ordering each sprint.
